Procedural Shader Collection

Unity Shader Graph & HLSL

A growing collection of procedural VFX shaders built in Unity — each one made to understand a specific technique rather than to ship a finished effect. This work grew directly out of my OpenGL renderer project; moving between low-level GLSL and Unity's Shader Graph/HLSL workflow has sharpened how I think about shaders generally, not just within one engine.

Below are two of the effects in motion. A third — a real-time color-changing material driven by shader parameters instead of material swaps — rounds out the current set.

Burn / Dissolve Transition

A procedural edge-burn transition — a common VFX technique I built to understand noise-driven alpha clipping. A noise texture drives a shrinking clip threshold, so the object dissolves with a glowing, irregular edge instead of a flat fade.

Sand Tornado Effect

A procedural particle and shader effect combining motion and material behavior.

This one leans more on motion than a single static shader — particles are driven by a swirling force field while a companion shader handles the sandy material response, so the effect reads as one cohesive tornado rather than particles and surface fighting each other.

Why shaders, alongside gameplay work: writing gameplay systems tells you what should happen on screen — shaders are where I get to control exactly how it looks while it's happening. This collection keeps growing as I pick up new techniques, usually pulled straight from whatever I'm currently exploring in the OpenGL renderer.
