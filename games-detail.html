Nightmares of the Creepy Cube

A 2D stealth-survival platformer where you play as a small cube escaping a nightmare world of hostile robots. I handled all programming, VFX, and music solo, focusing on tight movement and early level design.

The idea came after seeing Sqube Darkness on mobile — I wanted that same dark, moody tone but with deeper mechanics: motion-reactive enemies, destructible terrain, and stealth that rewards patience. It's built around three pillars — procedural generation, AI-driven hazards, and dynamic terrain destruction — all sharing one ground system in real time.

The Ground Generator uses Unity's Tilemap API to build terrain ahead of the player, tuned for randomness but constrained so no jump is impossible. Termites chase and self-destruct into the terrain; drones sweep the map with a vision-cone and fire homing missiles that carve holes on impact. PC controls and both enemy types are fully working — a handcrafted level mode and better onboarding are next.

Eyes on Duty

A mobile horror observation game inspired by the I'm on Observation Duty series — monitor camera feeds in a haunted restaurant and report anomalies before they spiral. I built the anomaly engine, camera system, and UI flow, all optimized for touch.

This was my first full mobile project — turning a simple idea into something complete and polished. The plan started simple, but I quickly focused on the systems underneath: unpredictable spawns, multiple anomaly types, and a difficulty curve that ramps naturally over a shift.

The AnomalyManager controls what spawns, where, and when, with each camera holding its own configuration. The AnomalyReportingSystem lets players pick a camera, choose a report type, and get instant feedback — all tuned for mobile with touch controls, balanced audio, and a light memory footprint. It works well as a proof of concept, with room to grow: more anomaly variety, livelier pacing, and subtler visual cues for sharp-eyed players.

Taxi Chaos

A 3D arcade-style taxi simulator in a low-poly open world — pick up NPC passengers, race them to their destination, and bank cash for speed and clean driving. I built every system from scratch: physics, AI traffic, missions, and rewards.

Arcade racers get pure adrenaline out of minimal mechanics — just you, the car, and a clock. Taxi Chaos mashes up Crazy Taxi's urgency with lightweight simulation: a low-poly city where AI traffic, pedestrians, and missions all coexist, scoped around responsive physics, AI pathing, a pickup/delivery loop, and rewards tied to speed and safety.

Vehicle control was rebuilt from scratch with separate input, motor, steering, and anti-roll scripts, tuned over many test drives. Instead of heavy NavMesh agents, AI traffic runs on a lightweight waypoint system so hundreds of cars run smoothly even on mid-tier hardware. AI still collides at intersections sometimes, and handling needs more nuance at speed — lane-switching, drift tuning, and a mobile release are next.
